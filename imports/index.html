Why the export totals differ

≈$1.896T is the project’s domestic-export series: U.S.-origin goods only. $2.063T is total Census-basis goods exports and includes re-exports. The gap is roughly the value of foreign-origin merchandise re-exported from the United States. For production analysis, domestic exports are the cleaner reference; for macro trade reporting, total exports remain useful.
Do not mix the two series in a trade-balance calculation. The dashboard keeps them visibly separate.

What can be said defensibly?

50.1% of 2024 goods imports are directly classifiable as capital goods + industrial supplies. Those two groups are the clearest production-oriented categories. Automotive, foods, and some consumer goods contain mixtures of intermediate and final use and should not be mechanically assigned without finer data.
Best current range: about 50–57% is defensible with high-to-medium confidence; a wider 55–65% range is plausible only after adding modeled shares of automotive and other mixed categories. The old 67% point estimate has been removed.
